---
description: "Deliver Integration Events and Snapshots from one Host to another through a RabbitMQ broker, with every guarantee of in-process messaging kept across it."
---

Inside one Host, the Kit delivers [Integration Events](/docs/reference/glossary#integration-event) and [Snapshots](/docs/reference/glossary#snapshot) through its message store. `PouryaKit.Messaging.Wolverine.RabbitMq` adds a broker between Hosts: each published [wire name](/docs/reference/glossary#wire-name) goes to a fanout exchange of its own, and each receiving Host has one durable queue, named by its [service name](/docs/reference/glossary#service-name), that fans each message out to its local [subscriptions](/docs/reference/glossary#subscription). From there a delivery is exactly what it is inside one Host: its own retries, [Receipt](/docs/reference/glossary#receipt) and dead letter.

Only the Hosts change. Modules, Contracts and Feature code never reference the package, and nothing in them changes when a Host adds it.

<Callout type="warn" title="Start a receiving Host before its first publication">
  A Host's queue and its bindings are declared when the Host starts, and the broker drops a message no queue is bound to. Start each Host that receives a wire name once before the first publication it must receive: on its first deployment, and on the one that adds its first subscription to a wire name.
</Callout>

## When to use it

- A Module runs in a Host of its own, a service, and receives another Host's Integration Events and Snapshots.
- You split a Module out of the modular monolith and keep its subscriptions working.
- You choose a Host's service name, or deploy a Host that receives.
- A receiving Host gets a body its contract cannot read, or you look for dead letters at its broker queue.
- You test two Hosts on one broker.

## How it works

<Steps>
<Step>

### The publishing Host joins the broker

After `UsePouryaKitWolverine`, the Host calls `UsePouryaKitRabbitMq` with its broker connection and its service name. The `Publish` and `PublishSnapshot` declarations its Modules already make decide every exchange it sends to.

</Step>
<Step>

### The receiving Host declares what it receives

A Host that subscribes to a message no Module of it publishes declares it with `Receive` or `ReceiveSnapshot`, under the wire name the publishing Module declares. The Host's queue is bound to that exchange, and its Modules subscribe as they always do.

</Step>
<Step>

### Each kind of Host keeps its own service name

Replicas of one Host share its queue as competing receivers. Two different Hosts must never share a name, or they would split its messages between them.

</Step>
</Steps>
